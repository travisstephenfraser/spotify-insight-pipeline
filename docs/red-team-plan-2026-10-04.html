Red Team: Pipeline Plan

Final assignment, spec stage. Six independent checkers tested 65 claims against the assignment's own files, the real data and vendor documentation. No model was run.

October 4, 2026

Verdict

Reshape. The core of the plan holds. Six findings change how it is sequenced, measured and built. This is a recommendation; the ruling is yours.

Each checker received plain claims and pointers to primary sources, never the plan itself. A claim marked Contradicted needed a quoted line, a measured count or a reproducible test, the same standard as Confirmed. Eight claims were planted with known wrong answers to check that the checkers read carefully; all eight came back correctly and are marked anchor below.

Six findings that change the plan

FindingEvidenceWhat changes
1. The race cannot double as the cost pilot COST_CALCULATOR.md line 23: "Include enrichment, a declared verification sample, grouping, ranking, and memo generation." Line 25: "Budget the pilot and any additional experiments before making calls." The race becomes a separately budgeted experiment with its own result cache. The official cold and warm pilot runs once all six stages exist.
2. Fifty labels cannot both pick a winner and grade it With 50 labels, a true 80% versus 70% gap is detected only 15% to 38% of the time; 132 to 313 labels are needed. Grading the winner on the labels that chose it inflates its score by 2 to 3 points. Any accuracy from 50 labels carries about plus or minus 11 points. Rare topics are nearly absent: a keyword proxy expects 0.9 account-access and 0.07 support reviews in 50, and the golden file has zero keyword matches for either. The brief (line 100) requires disclosure and fresh held-out cases when golden results shape the pipeline. Choose on a separate hand-labeled development set and score the golden 50 once, on the winner only. Or accept a direction-only choice and disclose it. Report per-topic counts as they are.
3. Jev through Cloudflare is not the fast option Cloudflare AI Gateway limits: "200 requests per 60 seconds per gateway". That is about 40 hours per pass over 484,189 texts and about 81 hours for two option orders. TypeSafe's direct API documents 80 requests per second, changeable without notice. Through Cloudflare, Jev suits small samples only. A full Jev run needs a direct TypeSafe key or an approved limit increase.
4. Jev's documented weak spot is this label type TypeSafe's limitations page: "something that requires multiple hops of reasoning costs accuracy", with advice to ask literal sub-questions and apply precedence in code. Its docs recommend Score, not Choice, for severity-like scales, while calling Score "weak in numerical calibration". Unreviewed preprints report about 58% on short five-way and six-way labels and a median 11.6-point deficit against the best LLM on 14 of 15 tasks. Identical requests do not always return identical answers, and the version cannot be pinned through Cloudflare. The first-option bias is asserted by the vendor only: three independent tests found order sensitivity (3% to 11% flips) but no first-position lean, and a two-order check misses most errors. The one-question-per-label Jev design is unlikely to apply the contract's "most severe problem, then first mentioned" rule. A fair Jev contender needs narrow yes/no questions with the rules in code. Option shuffling stays as a measurement, not a safeguard.
5. The checker punishes mixed configurations and revisiting finished reviews Fixture tests: a second model under its own label_config flags every such review (call_config_mismatch). Any enrich call marked resume that lists a review already in the first checkpoint is flagged, even a failed call (reprocessed_checkpoint). Calls without whole-number token counts are flagged, failed calls included. Checkpoints that list quarantined IDs fail. One invalid record cascades into ranking and claim mismatches. A review is saved as finished only when every call for it is done, and it is never sent again. Any fallback shares one label_config with the main model. Checkpoints list completed reviews only. The checker runs before memo numbers are final.
6. The data has traps the labels must handle Two review-bombing bursts: July 2023 (85,079 reviews, 70.5% one-star) and October 2023 (89,104). 3,484 reviews contain "boycott" and 7,450 contain "hate this application"; the contract labels bare slogans unclear. 12,268 nonempty reviews have no letter or digit (a thumbs-up appears 922 times) and must still be labeled. 13 reviews are the literal text "None". 2,094 duplicate groups carry both low and high stars. Write the slogan rule into the instructions and test it with planted cases. Emoji-only reviews get labels, never quarantine. Parse with Python's csv module only. Keep stars out of prompts and cache keys.

Two estimates corrected

Jev cost. The rate is $0.0441 per million tokens after Cloudflare's 5% billing fee. A short review is likely 730 to 820 tokens per request, not 600, and 1,300 to 1,700 with many sentence options. That gives roughly $16 to $18 per pass over the unique texts on the short-review figure, and more for long reviews. It stays an estimate until one real request is measured.
Cost sample. Reviews in cost_100.csv average 80 characters; unique texts average 99, because repeated texts are short. Scaling the pilot by review count would understate input size by about 18%. Compute text volume in code over the full file and use the pilot only for per-request overhead and output size.

What survived

  • Shuffled order. Sorting all rows by the manifest's hash rule puts the golden 50, the cost 100, the 500 and the 10,000 at the front in exact order. The development checkpoints are the front of the full-run queue.
  • Exact-text reuse. 484,189 distinct texts confirmed. Trimming whitespace merges nothing more. 93% of copies are 20 characters or fewer.
  • Sessions and resume. Five sessions with a checkpoint after session 1 and after session 5 pass the resume checks.
  • Two calls per review under one configuration pass, and duplicate copies pointing at one original pass in any file order.
  • Sentence splitting. Every piece is an exact copy of the source; the maximum is 164 pieces, under Jev's 255-option limit. 97.7% of single-piece reviews are under 200 characters.
  • Record shape. Whole-review quotes, empty entity lists and whole-number sentiment all pass.
  • Jev plumbing. Token counts come back with every response, and the logging switch is documented for the route in use.
  • Supplied files. All four samples match the full file byte for byte, and every checksum matches the manifest.

Not tested

  • Gemma's speed over an 8-hour session. Only a real run can answer it.
  • Label quality of either contender. Needs runs and human labels.
  • The Class 7 date. It is not in the assignment files, and the 500-review run, golden labels and working calculator are due then.
  • One real Jev request would settle the true token count, where usage sits in the Cloudflare response, and whether the logging switch works on this route.
  • Four questions only the instructor can answer: whether calls from the 100, 500 and 10,000 runs may appear in the final export under an identical configuration; whether one issue per topic is acceptable for the baseline ranking; whether one label_config may name a two-model procedure; and where side-experiment calls should be reported.

What would change the verdict

  • A direct TypeSafe key restores Jev's speed for a full run.
  • A labeled development set of roughly 130 to 220 reviews turns the race into a real test. With fewer, it shows a direction only.
  • A measured Jev run that applies the severity rule well on labeled reviews would overturn finding 4, which rests on documentation and unreviewed preprints.

Decisions for you, in order

  1. Jev's role: a small measured baseline, a rebuilt full contender, or dropped.
  2. Labels for choosing: hand-label a development set, or accept a direction-only choice and disclose it.
  3. Fallback model: none, or one that shares a configuration with the main model.
  4. Instructor questions: which of the four to send.
  5. Failed calls with unknown token counts: leave them missing and accept a checker flag, or record zero and explain it in the README.

Limits of this red team

  • No model ran, so nothing here measures label quality or speed.
  • The Jev accuracy figures come from unreviewed preprints and one public audit, all on version 1.13.0. The web checker's page fetches passed through a summarizer; quotes were taken from raw fetches.
  • Checkers were told to treat CLAUDE.md, which holds the plan, as the thing under test. One confirmed it never opened the file. The harness may still have loaded it for the others.
  • The single-annotator references in E5 were cited from memory, not re-fetched.
  • Side effect: the formatter hook created a .ruff_cache folder in the project root when checkers wrote scratch files. It is harmless and ignored by git.
  • The checkers' scripts and fixture tests sit in the session scratchpad (rt-checker, rt-stats, rt-data). That folder is temporary.
